# Data sources and validity

Airport-scene reference: **2026-09-06, Asia/Taipei (UTC+8)**. The project is a research-backed entertainment simulation, not an operational airport database. The scene still uses the AIRAC 03/26 geometry snapshot; CAA now lists AIRAC 05/26, effective **2026-10-29**, as the current issue. The newer airport data and live NOTAM changes have not been incorporated. A separate official flight-board snapshot is documented below.

## Official Taoyuan flight snapshot

The game also contains a fixed snapshot of the Taoyuan International Airport Corporation's [Taoyuan International Airport real-time flights dataset](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/26194), captured **2026-11-11 at 10:51:07 Asia/Taipei** from its [CSV resource](https://odp.taoyuan-airport.com/dataset/2023081816?format=csv). The source metadata lists five-minute updates and the **Open Government Data License, version 1.0**. The snapshot contains **1,208 records** (611 arrivals and 597 departures) from **62 IATA carrier codes**. It preserves the published flight number, direction, destination/origin, scheduled and expected times, terminal, gate, status, and reported aircraft model. The source lists 636 aircraft models and leaves 572 blank.

The archived snapshot is used only when the scenario date is **2026-11-11**. Other dates continue to use the explicitly labeled deterministic synthetic schedule. There is no runtime network request. When the official feed has no aircraft model, the simulator uses an A320-size proxy so the flight remains controllable; a published model is displayed as reported, while the nearest existing family profile drives simulation geometry and movement. The 777-family proxy is used for the two A380-861 records. A reported gate is preserved; where the scene's stand set or capacity cannot match it, the simulator uses a compatible stand and shows that mapping. The snapshot does not provide a certified runway assignment, taxi clearance, or aircraft track, so those operations remain simulated. The four existing custom liveries remain bespoke original approximations, and the 58 added carriers now have their own procedural standard-paint interpretations; they are not exact aircraft paint kits. Existing carriers have configured radio telephony names; for new carriers, spoken radio falls back to spelling the IATA code because the official telephony designators have not yet been verified.

Attribution: **Taoyuan International Airport Corporation Ltd., “Taoyuan International Airport real-time flights,” snapshot 2026-11-11 10:51:07 (Asia/Taipei).** Reuse is under the [Open Government Data License, version 1.0](https://data.gov.tw/en/license). The portal describes the live dataset as updated every five minutes; this bundled file is a static dated snapshot, not a live feed.

## 2026 construction and tower

The [Executive Yuan's 2025-11-08 airport inspection report](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/9277F759E41CCD91/73d10376-1ac1-4469-b359-2b56f1da0cc8) distinguishes the north concourse opening from main-terminal/south-concourse works planned through 2027. The [National Immigration Agency report dated 2025-12-04](https://news.immigration.gov.tw/NewsSection/Detail/8abf4889-4a4d-4457-82c0-b5d216547a4d?category=1&lang=TW) confirms north-concourse trial operation began 2025-12-01. The applicable CAA chart and stand list include D11–D18. Together these support an operating north concourse and a visibly incomplete T3 main/south area; exact progress on 2026-09-06 is not verified. Earlier 2022–23 completion forecasts were not treated as achieved milestones.

The [ANWS tower project description](https://www.anws.gov.tw/OpenInformation/ApproachTower/Plan.htm) distinguishes **62 m structure / 65 m including antenna**. The [ANWS 2019 annual report](https://www.anws.gov.tw/FileDownLoad/Publications/20200716015946125/30/) dates completion to 2019-11-20 and the new park opening to December 2019. The tower position is approximated from its OSM footprint; its cab size/interior are original functional interpretations. [ANWS tower automation documentation](https://www.anws.gov.tw/OpenInformation/ApproachTower/Tower.htm) supports surface surveillance, weather, flight-data and docking integration as design references. No proprietary interface has been reproduced.

All checked on 2026-09-06; product pages without dated revisions are recorded as undated. Manufacturer dimensions support recognizable scale, not aircraft-specific certified performance:

- [Airbus A320neo](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/aircraft/a320-family/a320neo): length 37.57 m; span 35.80 m.
- [Airbus A321neo](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/aircraft/a320-family/a321neo): length 44.51 m; span 35.80 m.
- [Airbus A330-300](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/aircraft/a330/a330-300): length 63.66 m; span 60.30 m.

- [Boeing 787](https://www.boeing.com/commercial/787/): 787-9 length 62.8 m; 787-10 length 68.3 m; both spans 60.1 m. Extra decimal places in the model profile are scale parameters rather than a claim of greater source precision.

- [Boeing airport compatibility brochure](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/airports/acaps/777_8F_Brochure.pdf): comparison table identifies the existing 777F's 63.7 m length and 64.8 m span. The future 777-8F column is not substituted for 777F.

Taxi speeds, thrust/weight response, braking, V1/VR, rotation, wake timing and turn radii are gameplay profiles. They are not published flight-manual values or instructions for operating real aircraft. Reference weights may differ by certified variant; see REALISM_NOTES.md.

## Aircraft nose and mapped landside research (2026-10-28)

The procedural nose and cockpit geometry was revised against photographs: the [A350 nose reference](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Airbus_A-350_XWB_F-WWYB_nose_cone.jpg) shows the dark surround and three panes on each side; the [787 cockpit reference](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boeing_787_Dreamliner_Cockpit.jpg) shows two on each side. Reference photographs were used for observation only and are not included as game assets. Each family has an independently sized rounded nose and mirrored forward glazing. Shapes, colour and surface detail remain artistic approximations; no engineering drawings are claimed.

The [Taoyuan International Airport corporation's area layout, revised 2026-03-02](https://www.taoyuanairport.com.tw/api/imagecrop/fileid/1E52490A-BE12-F111-BC22-0050569094FE), distinguishes runway/taxiway grass, terminal aprons, cargo, maintenance and landside areas. Existing grass between operational pavement was intentionally retained. [Taiwan Freeway Bureau's W1/W2 project description](https://www.freeway.gov.tw/Upload/Html/2015514112/page06.html) describes the taxiway bridges crossing Terminal North Road, Terminal South Road and associated routes; mapped tunnels are not rendered as surface roads. The [Bureau's National Highway 2 improvement record](https://www.freeway.gov.tw/Print.aspx?cnid=195&p=30749) also describes improvements to these roads. These references do not establish a survey-grade scene or exact construction progress for every date.

Landside and apron outlines use **© OpenStreetMap contributors**, available under the [Open Database License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The game's always-visible attribution links to that page; the derived, anonymized feature/node dataset and provenance manifest are available locally at `assets/geography/taoyuan-osm-source.json` and `assets/geography/taoyuan-osm-manifest.json`. No contributor names or account data are retained. The runtime subset contains 454 road ways, 17 apron ways and 29 parking ways before render-time exclusions. The airport's existing aircraft graph is unchanged.

The data were retrieved on **2026-10-28** from the public [OSM API](https://api.openstreetmap.org/) in 12 tiles covering latitude 25.055–25.115 and longitude 121.195–121.275. Only ways whose latest current version and all referenced current node versions were last edited no later than **2026-09-06 04:00 UTC** were retained. This is an intentionally **incomplete subset of unchanged current objects**, not a historical snapshot: deleted objects and objects subsequently edited cannot be recovered by this filter. Consequently it does not prove every rendered feature existed exactly in the game scenario and omissions are expected. The importer `scripts/import-osm-airport.py` records tile checksums and projects the coordinates using the same local basis as the airport model. Public surface motor roads are shown; mapped underground/covered ways are omitted, and road strips are clipped near the simulator's runway and taxi corridors because the operational graph and building shapes are approximate. Road widths without reliable OSM width/lanes are visual defaults. Parking outlines are surface representations, not surveyed parking bays. The surrounding fields and buildings remain generalized scenery. Roads are visual only and do not change aircraft collision or taxi-routing logic.
# Northwest coastal scenery (visual only)

The Taoyuan-region coastline uses OpenStreetMap `natural=coastline` ways in the bounding box 24.65–25.35° N, 120.72–121.65° E, requested from the public Overpass Kumi endpoint on 2026-10-28. The endpoint reported an OSM snapshot of 2026-07-15 15:22:01 UTC; it is not a current or certified shoreline. The largest connected mainland chain comprises 102 ways and 2,393 coordinate points. It is projected into the airport's local tangent plane and simplified to 18 m for visual rendering; offshore islands are omitted. Outside the downloaded chain the sea boundary is extended as an approximation, not a mapped representation of all Taiwan.

Original query output is retained in `public/assets/geography/taoyuan-coast-source.json`; `scripts/import-coastline.py` reproduces `src/data/coastline.json`. The coastline, sea shading, and land/sea scenery filter are visual-only and do not alter aircraft routing or represent navigational charts. Data: © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL and attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).

The ground-service illustrations include passenger baggage handling and a 777F main-deck loader, informed by [Boeing's 777-200LR / 300ER / Freighter airport planning guide](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/airports/acaps/777-200LR-300ER-F_Rev_G.pdf). People, equipment proportions, timing and marshalling gestures are visual approximations, not certified operating procedures. They do not simulate cargo quantities or control aircraft movements.

## Tower campus and parking areas between terminals

The [ANWS tower campus design description](https://www.anws.gov.tw/OpenInformation/ApproachTower/concept.htm) identifies the tower site between the terminals and its flight-service, standby and power / air-conditioning areas. Building footprints use OSM ways 758856658–758856663; parking areas use ways 24000909 and 395947062 from the landside subset. The tower centre is approximately aligned to its mapped footprint. `scripts/import-terminal-core.py` extracts these objects from the same downloaded OSM tiles, with the unchanged-object cutoff and historical limitations described above; `assets/geography/terminal-core-osm-source.json` records their IDs, versions, timestamps and projected coordinates. Data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

The campus heights, roof details, glazing, parking stalls and parked cars are procedural estimates; they are not surveyed or a claim about exact present-day occupancy. Mapped surface roads are kept clear of these parking details, and mapped tunnels are not drawn as surface roads. The existing terminal buildings and concourses remain simplified, so the airport scene as a whole is not a complete or survey-accurate reconstruction.

## Regional terrain and distant landscape

Visual terrain uses [Mapzen Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/), downloaded 2026-11-10 from the public AWS Terrarium dataset at zoom 10. The global sources include SRTM and GMTED2010 (terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey) and ETOPO1 (U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration). See the [dataset attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md). The tile URLs and SHA-256 checksums are in `assets/geography/taoyuan-terrain-manifest.json`; `scripts/import-terrain.py` resamples them to a 400 m grid in the simulator's local coordinate system. Heights are rounded, shifted by an approximate airport reference elevation, flattened around the airport and ocean, and blended at dataset edges. This is a visual approximation, not surveyed terrain or navigation data; the ground does not affect aircraft simulation.

Major roads, rivers and settlement centres come from © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), using an Overpass snapshot dated 2026-06-01 08:52:28 UTC. The derived dataset is available at `assets/geography/taoyuan-regional-osm.json`, and its importer is `scripts/import-regional-osm.py`. Surface ways are simplified, clipped away from the airport, and drawn with estimated widths; tunnels are omitted. The city buildings are procedurally distributed near mapped settlement centres, and fields and vegetation are invented scenery rather than aerial imagery or surveyed land use. Coastline, road alignment, buildings, trees, weather, seasonal changes and heights may differ from the real landscape.

## Terminal 3 completed-design visualization

At the player's request, the main terminal and south concourse are rendered as an approximate completed-design preview, even though the 2026 operational scenario retains their construction status. According to the [airport corporation's 2026 progress report](https://web.taoyuan-airport.com/news/7D798237-4C69-F111-BC28-0050569094FE), the main terminal roof and glass curtain wall were completed in April 2026 and overall completion was targeted for 2027. The [National Development Council](https://www.ndc.gov.tw/nc_27_37364) describes the main building as approximately 414 by 242 m, with a large wave roof and 16 supporting columns. The [designers' project description](https://www.arup.com/en-us/projects/taoyuan-international-airport-terminal-3/) describes the roof and structural concept. The modeled roof profile, glazing, concourse, connectors and positioning are visual interpretations, not exact architectural drawings. No future gates, routes or airline operations have been added.
